Most rain garden guidance is written for places that get slow, steady rain. The Vail Valley doesn't work that way. We get long dry stretches punctuated by short, intense monsoon storms and rapid spring snowmelt — two very different water events that a single basin has to handle.

## Sizing for snowmelt, not just storms

A basin sized only for a 2-inch summer storm will overflow every spring when snowmelt sends a steady volume of water downhill for weeks at a time. That means factoring in a sustained base flow, not just peak storm intake, when setting basin depth and overflow elevation.

## Soil amendments matter more here

Native soils in much of the valley are thin and rocky, sitting over decomposed granite or bedrock that doesn't infiltrate well on its own. A functional basin usually needs an engineered soil mix — sharp sand, compost, and enough depth to actually hold and slowly release water rather than sheeting off the surface.

## Plant selection follows the hydrology

Once the basin is sized and the soil mix is right, plant choice is straightforward: species that can handle brief inundation in spring and complete drought by August. Golden currant, red-twig dogwood, and native sedges do well in the basin floor, with more drought-tolerant xeric species on the basin's outer slopes where it stays drier.

The result is a feature that actually functions in April *and* August, instead of a pretty basin that either floods or dries to cracked mud depending on the season.
