Clients often come to a first meeting ready to talk about plants. That conversation happens eventually, but the first visit is about something less visual: where the water goes, where the sun actually falls, and what's happening underground.

## Grading and drainage first

Every site has a story about where runoff goes during a heavy storm — off a roofline, down a driveway, across a patio. Walking the property after a rain (or asking the client where puddles tend to form) tells you more about the eventual design than any style preference does.

## Soil, not assumptions

A quick soil check — texture, compaction, depth to rock — determines whether infiltration-based solutions like a rain garden are realistic on a given site, or whether the plan needs to lean more on surface drainage and detention instead. Skipping this step is how expensive features end up failing within a year.

## Sun and microclimate

South-facing slopes, wind exposure, and shade from structures or existing trees all shift which xeric species will actually thrive in a given bed. Two yards a block apart can call for different plant lists once you account for their microclimates.

Only once those three things are understood does the planting and layout conversation start — at that point, it's a much shorter conversation, because the site has already ruled out the options that wouldn't have worked anyway.
