## The brief

The HOA board wanted to cut irrigation costs across a quarter-acre common area without eliminating the lawn entirely — residents still wanted a usable green space for kids and dogs.

## The approach

Roughly two-thirds of the existing bluegrass turf was converted to xeric planting beds and native grass meadow, with a smaller, well-defined lawn panel retained at the center for actual use. The irrigation system was redesigned around zones rather than running the whole area on one schedule, so the retained turf and the xeric beds each get water suited to what they need.

## Planting

Meadow mix of blue grama and buffalo grass at the perimeter, transitioning into structured beds of little bluestem, penstemon, and yarrow near the walking path, with rabbitbrush massed at the property edge for fall color.

## Result

Irrigation runtime across the common area dropped by roughly 45% in the first full season, and the HOA board used the before/after water bill as the basis for approving similar conversions elsewhere in the development.
