## The problem

A steep, south-facing slope below a newly built home was actively eroding after grading disturbed the original vegetation. The client's contractor had priced a full retaining wall system, but the grade didn't strictly require one — it needed root structure and surface stabilization.

## The approach

Instead of a wall, the plan uses a series of low, informal terraces built with buried rock check-dams to slow surface flow, combined with a planting palette chosen specifically for deep, erosion-controlling root systems: little bluestem, big sagebrush, and native wildflower seed mix broadcast across jute erosion-control fabric.

## Establishment

The first season used temporary drip irrigation to get plants established; by the second season the slope was weaned off irrigation entirely, relying on root depth and natural precipitation.

## Result

The slope has held through two full runoff seasons with no visible erosion, at roughly a third of the cost of the originally quoted retaining wall system.
